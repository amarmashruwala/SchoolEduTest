## Vocabulary
### VOC-1.1
term: inference
meaning: A conclusion reached from evidence and reasoning rather than from explicit statements.
source: demo://chapter-1/para-1
example: The inference that Maya expected rain came from her packing an umbrella.

### VOC-1.2
term: textual evidence
meaning: Specific words, phrases, or details from the text that support an idea or inference.
source: demo://chapter-1/para-2
example: "Maya looked at the dark clouds" is textual evidence for why she packed an umbrella.

### VOC-1.3
term: motivation
meaning: The reason why a character does something; what drives their actions.
source: demo://chapter-1/para-3
example: Leo's motivation for checking under the bed was worry, even though he said he wasn't scared.

## Summary and flashcards
Key idea: Authors leave clues (actions, dialogue, descriptions) before a character's choice. Readers use these clues to make inferences about motivations.
Flashcard 1: Q: What is an inference? A: A conclusion from evidence and reasoning, not explicit statements.
Flashcard 2: Q: Where do you find clues about a character's choice? A: In the text right before the choice — what they notice, say, or do.
Flashcard 3: Q: What if words and actions don't match? A: Actions often reveal the true feeling.

## Homework and revision
1. Read a short story of your choice. Pick one character choice and write down two textual clues that explain it.
2. Find an example where a character's words and actions differ. What do you infer?
